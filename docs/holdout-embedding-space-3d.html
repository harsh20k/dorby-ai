What this is

Every contact in the frozen 69-pair real holdout appears twice over. Once as an anchor — their whole profile, serialised exactly as the baselines serialise it, with the search query deliberately left out. And once per lookingFor section — the same full profile with lookingFor swapped down to a single one of its asks. That is the precise text variant the sectioning experiment scores against, so this map shows the geometry that experiment is actually operating on.

All 1,066 vectors were encoded in one voyage-4-nano pass on an L4, then projected together through a single PCA so that a section’s offset from its own anchor is drawn on the same scale as the gap between two different contacts.

The thing the map shows

Sectioning barely moves a profile. A section vector sits at a mean cosine of to its own anchor, while two different contacts sit at . Swapping lookingFor down to one ask changes a profile’s position by a rounding error next to the distance separating one person from another — a contact’s sections form a tight knot roughly the radius of the cloud they sit in. At 1.0× spread the constellations are almost invisible; that is the honest picture, and the slider only inflates the offsets so the tether structure can be read.

The reason is structural. A section variant keeps the entire rest of the profile — positioning, background, notes, preferences — and edits one field. The shared text dominates the embedding, so every section inherits almost all of its coordinates from the profile it came out of.

Why it matters for the sectioning result

Seeker-sectioning measurably helps at the top of the ranking — top-1 retrieval went from 27.6% to 34.5% — and it does that on offsets this small. The signal it adds is real but it is a fine adjustment layered on a position that is set almost entirely by the rest of the profile, not a relocation of the contact.

That also frames the Recall@10 question. If every section sits within cosine of its anchor, the max-over-sections score is being taken over a set of very similar candidates, and which one wins is decided by small differences. Softening the aggregation could not recover Recall@10 partly because there was never much spread to aggregate over.

Do vague profiles get worse matches?

A contact whose sections disagree with each other is carrying several unrelated asks at once. Call that dispersion — one minus the mean cosine among a contact’s own sections. It is worth measuring separately from sheer section count, and it is: the two are almost uncorrelated (r = 0.06), so dispersion tracks breadth of intent rather than volume of text.

Asked directly — does dispersion tell you whether a pair was labelled good or bad? — the answer is no. Across both sides of the pair and four different shape measures, every ROC-AUC lands between and , and nothing survives a permutation test. That is the sensible result: the label describes a relationship between two people, so a property of one profile on its own has no particular reason to predict it.

Asked the other way — does dispersion predict how hard a seeker is to serve? — there is a real effect. Ranking each positive pair’s true match against all holdout candidates, a seeker’s dispersion correlates with how far down their true match falls at (Spearman, permutation , 95% CI ). It holds at after controlling for section count, so this is not just “longer profiles are harder”. Scattered seekers are harder to match — not more likely to be given a bad match, but more likely to have their right match buried.

Essentially all of sectioning’s benefit goes to the multi-intent seekers. Focused seekers gain nothing, because there was nothing blurred together to separate. This is the mechanism the earlier follow-ups were reaching for: sectioning is not a general improvement to the encoder, it is a targeted repair for profiles that carry several live threads at once — which also predicts it would be wasted effort on single-topic fields like locationAvailability.

Sample-size caution: 29 positive queries, split 17 / 12. The correlation’s bootstrap interval reaches close to zero at the low end and this was one of several measures examined, so treat it as a well-formed lead rather than a settled number.
