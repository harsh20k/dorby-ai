Experiment · dorby-ai · 2026-07-29

We trained the MoE on 2,619 synthetic pairs. It got worse.

24× more training data made every model lose to using no model at all. The useful part is where it broke: the synthetic vocabulary transfers almost perfectly, and the labels don't transfer at all.

Answer

No. Every arm trained on synthetic pairs scored below the no-model TF-IDF floor of 0.5660. Two scored below chance.

Held up

One prediction held. Training within a seeker instead of on raw pairs was worth +0.131 — the largest single effect measured, and the one thing the synthetic batch genuinely unlocked.

Corrects

The earlier conclusion was wrong and is now corrected. The bottleneck is not data volume — it is label validity. A teacher scoring 0.5797 against real human outcomes cannot teach a student to beat plain keyword cosine.

ResultsEvery arm, scored on real pairs

DiagnosisWhere exactly it broke

A synthetic-to-real failure can happen in two places: the text could look different, or the labels could mean something different. These separate cleanly here.

The vocabulary transfers

A TF-IDF vocabulary learned entirely from synthetic profiles scores real pairs at 0.5631 — within 0.003 of one learned from real profiles (0.5660). The synthetic profiles are lexically fine.

The labels do not

Models learn the synthetic labels well (0.61–0.66 synthetic-internal AUC) and then score 0.41–0.54 on real pairs. Fitting works; the meaning of "good match" does not survive the move.

Below chance is a real signal

Two arms land at 0.4066 and 0.4270. That is not "no signal" — the feature→label relationship learned from synthetic pairs is anti-correlated with real outcomes. Training on it inverts the decision.

ImplicationThe distillation ceiling

This is the number that reframes the whole plan. Distillation only works if the teacher is worth imitating, so: how good is the judge itself on real pairs?

Signal is lost at every hop. The student learns the judge's labels well. The judge is only weakly right about humans. And the student ends up below even that — because it learns the judge's decision function as expressed on synthetic profiles, which is not the function the judge applies to real ones. Distilling on synthetic data does not even reproduce the judge's own real-pair behaviour.

The consequence: a perfect imitator of this judge would still score below what plain logistic regression already achieves on 105 real pairs. More labels at this teacher quality is not the lever. The judge has to get better first — and structured_cot already failed at that.

Positive resultWhat did work

CorrectionsTwo bugs caught mid-run

Both would have produced confidently wrong conclusions, and both were caught by a number looking implausible rather than by a test failing.

Bug 1Fixed

A hand-rolled TF-IDF that was simply wrong

The feature module first built its own vectorizer with unigrams and sublinear scaling, against the repo encoder's bigrams and no sublinear scaling. On the same 131 real pairs that scored 0.4366 versus the repo's 0.5660, correlation only 0.52.

Why it mattered TF-IDF is the strongest single channel in the entire feature set. Getting it wrong would have made every downstream number untrustworthy — and it would have looked like a finding about synthetic data.
Fixed by using the repo's TfidfEncoder unchanged, verified by reproducing the documented holdout figure (0.5905 vs 0.5922 published). Reuse beat re-derivation.
Bug 2Fixed

A stale cache silently merged two arms into one

TfidfEncoder.encode() keys its disk cache on the texts, not on the fitted vocabulary. This experiment deliberately encodes the same real rows under two different fits — real vocabulary and synthetic vocabulary — so they collided, and the second call returned the first call's vectors.

The tell The two arms reported byte-identical AUCs of 0.5660. A coincidence too exact to be real. Had it gone unnoticed, the headline "vocabulary transfers perfectly" would have been an artifact instead of a finding.
Fixed by calling vectorizer.transform directly, bypassing the cache. Worth knowing for any future experiment that re-encodes identical text under a new fit — the project notes flag this hazard for explicit cache names, but it applies to the content-hashed default too.

LimitsCaveats, stated plainly

  • The CV arms are very noisy. Fold-to-fold standard deviations of 0.145–0.177 over 5 folds. logistic_real and moe_real differ by more than that spread, but neither is a precise estimate.
  • "Does not beat the floor" is safe; "is worse than the floor" is not. The single-shot arms carry SE ±0.0505, so 0.5380 and 0.5660 are not cleanly separable.
  • The feature set is weaker than the previous experiment's. The embedding channel had to be dropped — rrf_003's cached vectors are Qwen3, the real-pair features are voyage-nano, and mixing spaces would make a feature mean different things per row. Absolute levels are therefore lower across the board; the comparison is unaffected since every arm shares it.
  • The real 69-pair holdout was not spent. Nothing came close to earning the one-shot check.
  • These labels were never claimed to be ground truth. The value here is quantifying by how much they fail and locating where — which turned out to be the labeling function, not the profile text.

DecisionWhat to carry forward

Keep
  • Within-seeker training. +0.131, validated, cheap.
  • The 200 real pairs as the only trustworthy signal. Simple models on them remain the strongest thing measured in this project.
  • The frozen-copy discipline. Source hash verified unchanged; the synthetic batch is reusable without being disturbed.
Drop or rethink
  • Scaling judge-labeled data. More pairs at 0.5797 teacher quality is not the lever.
  • Synthetic pretraining. It is a liability the fine-tune cannot undo (0.4730 vs 0.6398).
  • "The bottleneck is data." It is label validity. 24× more pairs made things worse.
