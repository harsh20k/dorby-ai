nomad_drift_sectioned — follow-up to nomad_drift

Splitting the profile into asks beats blending in the whole thing

In plain words: instead of mixing a seeker's entire biography into the search, pick out just the one paragraph of their profile that’s actually about what they’re asking for right now, and mix in only that. It beats mixing in the whole profile on almost every measure — but the calibration method used to pick the mix ratio couldn’t actually detect the improvement, which is its own useful lesson.

Plain-language summary: a seeker's profile often has several separate asks going at once ("I want investors" / "I want a co-founder" / "I want customers"). The old approach averaged all of them into one vector before mixing in the query, which waters down whichever one the query is actually about. This experiment picks out just the relevant paragraph first. Result: better at finding the right person, better at telling real matches from fake ones, at almost every mixing ratio tested — new best scores on 4 of 5 metrics measured on all 200 real pairs.

The honest catchThe calibration never actually saw this

The mixing ratio was tuned on a separate, 13x-bigger set of made-up practice profiles before ever touching real data (same method as nomad_drift). That tuning process picked "100% query, 0% profile" as the best ratio — and at 100% query, it no longer matters at all whether the leftover 0% is the whole profile or one section, because it's multiplied by zero either way. The improvement above only shows up in the middle of the ratio range (30-80% query), which the tuning process was never able to distinguish. It's a real result, just one the automated tuning step couldn't have found on its own — it took looking at the two full result tables side by side.

A second honest catch: picking which section is "the relevant one" (by comparing it to the query) was only right 48.8% of the time against the practice data's known correct answer — barely better than a coin flip. It still helped on real data anyway. That's a genuinely surprising result worth sitting with, not a clean story: a fairly unreliable selection step still moved every metric in the right direction.
