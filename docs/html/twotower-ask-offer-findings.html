Two Towers, One Verdict

Two separately-trained LoRA towers, optimized jointly on the reciprocal score — run end to end. It didn’t beat the reigning model, and the reason why is the real finding.

Headline: on the all-200 population, the reciprocal term adds real signal (pair AUC 0.513→0.571). On the 69-pair holdout it does nothing (0.614→0.612). Neither beats voyage_gemini_ctrl’s combined score (0.659 all-200 / 0.735 holdout) — one shared tower, full-profile text, reciprocal term applied for free with zero extra training.

What it means

Three things worth carrying forward

  • The reciprocal term itself works — on the population this project trusts more (all-200, not the noisy 69-pair holdout), combining forward + reverse score adds real accuracy, same direction as the zero-training version of this idea.
  • The trained forward tower is the weak link. 0.513 all-200 AUC is barely above chance, and below frozen Voyage-4-nano’s own forward score (0.564) with zero training. Narrowing each tower to one field (lookingFor / positioning+background) likely cost more than untying the weights gained.
  • Two variables changed at once — untying the towers and narrowing the text both happened in this run. This result can’t tell them apart; a follow-up that changes only one would.
