Design review · dorby-ai · 2026-07-29

A heavy MoE re-ranker over the lookingFor field

The two-stage shape is right, and splitting the seeker's asks is the best-proven trick in this project. But the five experts as drawn have nothing to learn from, and the repo already ran the weighted-average version of this idea and lost.

✅

Keep: retrieve-then-rerank, and exploding the seeker's lookingFor into sections. Both are backed by real numbers here.

🔴

Change: the five named experts have zero labels in the real data, and "weighted combine" is the wrong way to add up expert opinions — real declines look like a veto, not an average.

🟡

Do first: two cheap tests (one free, one ~$0.05) that tell you whether the whole premise holds — before writing any model code.

01What's genuinely right about it

Rerank a shortlist, don't rescore the pool

This is the only way to spend real compute without putting a heavy model on the fast lookup path. Correct answer to the 100 ms budget.

Splitting the seeker's asks works

Already measured: it lifted pair AUC 0.579 → 0.596 and top-1 hits 27.6% → 37.9%. Splitting the candidate side lost on every metric. Your diagram splits the right side.

You already have the right retriever

The bigbatch triplet run trades accuracy for recall (AUC 0.610 → 0.576). That's a weak final scorer and a good candidate generator. Stop reading its AUC drop as a failure.

In plain words: a seeker usually has several unrelated things going on at once — fundraising, hiring, finding customers. Squashing all of that into one embedding blurs them together. Any one intro is only ever about one of those threads, so letting that thread drive the score instead of the blur is a genuine win. That part of your idea is not speculation; it's the thing that already worked.

02The one chart that should worry you most

Why this matters for your design: your gate reads the seeker's lookingFor and nothing else. That is exactly the input needed to learn "this seeker says no to everything" — a shortcut that scores higher than every real model in the project. A gate will find it before it finds anything about side, stage, or role.

03Problems, worst first

Problem 1 Fatal as drawn

The five experts have nothing to learn from

Each expert is named after a failure mode — wrong side, wrong stage, wrong role, geo mismatch, prefs conflict. Those names come from the synthetic negative generator's recipe: a list of ways to deliberately build a bad pair. They are not something anyone observed in the real data.

Checked in the data 0 of 100 real negatives carry a failure_mode. So do 339 of 340 negatives in dataset_negative.json — the labels only survive in the staging envelope (artifacts/synth/*/staged/, ~55 per mode), and promote.py drops them.

Real negatives are intros production recommended and humans declined, with no reason recorded. So you'd train the experts on hand-built mismatches, then apply them to a population that may not break down that way at all. Nobody has checked whether real declines even have five buckets.

Fix: test the taxonomy before building anything on it (Step 1).
Problem 2 Already tried, already lost

"Weighted combine" is the part that failed last time

The structured_cot judge scores six weighted aspects with evidence and adds them up in code. That is your architecture, with an LLM as every expert and the gate weights set by hand. It lost on every single metric.

Why it failed, and why it transfers: averaging several independent sub-scores pulls everything toward the middle. A pair with one fatal flaw and five perfectly fine aspects still lands near 0.5, so it gets called a match. You can see it in the yes-rate jumping from 55% to 75% — it got less decisive, not more.

Real declines are almost certainly veto-shaped. One dealbreaker kills an intro; five nice-to-haves don't rescue it. A weighted sum cannot express that.

Fix — my single biggest change to your diagram: the combine step should be soft-min or noisy-OR (worst expert dominates), not a weighted average.
Problem 3 High

The gate will learn the seeker's mood, not the matching axes

See the chart above: seeker identity alone scores 0.687, higher than every real model. Your gate is fed the seeker and only the seeker, so that shortcut is the easiest thing in its reach.

Budget reality 131 real training pairs. One bit of supervision each (accepted / declined) — versus a gate plus five fresh expert heads. Gate collapse (all weight to one expert) is the default outcome here, not a risk to monitor.
Fix: gate on the section × candidate interaction, never the seeker alone; and train within a seeker (249 triplets exist), which cancels the per-seeker base rate by construction.
Problem 4 High

The sections you get aren't the experts you want

Exploding lookingFor gives you topics. Your experts are failure axes. Those are two different ways to cut the problem, and the gate would have to translate between them with no supervision on 131 pairs.

Actual section headers in the real pairs fundraising (67) · hiring (31) · customers (24) · partnerships (20) · founders (18) · build clients (15) · geography (11) · accelerators (10)
Not: wrong_side · wrong_stage · wrong_role · geo_mismatch · prefs_conflict
Problem 5 Unmeasured

"Heavy" needs an actual number, and none exists

Cost is candidates × sections forward passes. At 50 candidates and a typical 4 sections, that's ~200 passes on the live path. The project rules out scoring each pair with a cross-encoder online — and there is still no latency benchmark anywhere in this repo. This is the one thing you cannot reason your way to.

Fix that keeps your shape: experts consume precomputed candidate vectors, so the candidate side stays offline and the live cost is a few section encodes plus cheap little MLP heads. That's a late-interaction re-ranker, not a cross-encoder — and it fits the budget.
Problem 6 Measurement

You can't detect a win this small on 69 pairs

The holdout is 69 pairs, 29 of them positive, and every model ever tested lands between 0.578 and 0.660. A five-expert MoE has enough knobs that the most likely outcome is picking a winner out of noise.

Fix: write down the win threshold before running, use a within-seeker metric on train-dev while iterating, and keep the real holdout a one-shot final check.

04The same idea, redrawn

Your diagram
  • Gate reads seeker's lookingFor only
  • Five experts named after synthetic failure modes
  • Weighted combine → match score
  • Trained pairwise on accept/decline
  • Heavy scoring of each (seeker, candidate) pair online
What survives the evidence
  • Gate reads the section × candidate interaction — never the seeker alone
  • Experts kept only if each beats chance standalone (Step 2)
  • Soft-min / noisy-OR → one bad axis vetoes the match
  • Trained listwise within a seeker (249 triplets)
  • Candidate vectors precomputed offline; live cost = section encodes + MLP heads

05How I'd sequence it

Both of the first two steps are cheap and either one can kill the idea. Do them before writing model code.

0

Change the way you add up the sections free · today

The per-section similarities are already computed and cached. Add min and noisy-OR next to the existing max (0.596) and softmax (0.598). If veto-shaped adding-up beats max, your premise gains real support with zero new model. If min does nothing, the veto story is wrong and I'd drop the MoE.

1

Ask what real declines actually look like ~$0.05 · highest value

Have the naive judge — the best hard-negative scorer in the project, and the only model where hard negatives score better than easy ones — write a free-text reason for each of the 100 real declines, then cluster them. Do they fall into five buckets? Are they these five? If most come back "nothing's wrong, just not compelling," there are no axes to model and Steps 2–3 are moot.

2

Prove each expert alone before wiring a gate cheap

Geo is nearly a rule off locationAvailability; stage and role come off positioning. Score each axis on its own against the real holdout. An expert that can't beat a coin flip alone will not be rescued by a gate.

3

Only now, build it gated on 0–2 passing

Interaction-gating, soft-min combine, listwise within-seeker training, precomputed candidate vectors, and one single shot at the real holdout.

06What I'd bet on instead

Distill the LLM judge into the re-ranker. You already have a teacher at 0.641 AUC with the best hard-negative performance in the project — and hard negatives are the only population that exists in production. You can generate its labels at scale, which is exactly what you can't do for the five failure axes. A small late-interaction student trained to copy the judge's score gets you the same goal using labels you can actually make. The expert decomposition can go inside that student later if you still want it.

⚖️

One honest point for the MoE: structured_cot bought explainability but not accuracy. If what you actually need to ship is "here's why we recommend this intro," the MoE may be worth building even at flat accuracy. Just be clear with yourself which of the two you're buying — the evidence says you probably can't have both.
