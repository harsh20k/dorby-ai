The holdout ranks bad models fine, and good models not at all

Fourteen models re-scored on all 200 real pairs instead of 69. The leaderboard changed — but the reason it changed is the actual finding.

Every baseline number in this project has been computed on a frozen 69-pair holdout: 29 positive queries ranked against 65 candidates. That population had already reversed a conclusion three times. So all fourteen models were re-scored on the full 200 real pairs — 100 positive queries, a 178-candidate corpus, 2.9× the sample.

Rank the models by MRR on each population and correlate the two orderings. Taken as a whole, the holdout looks excellent:

The headline +0.886 is carried almost entirely by the bottom of the table. Among the six best models the holdout carries no ranking information whatsoever — and those six span just 0.052 MRR.

Why this is the worst possible place to fail

Nobody in this project has ever needed to know whether zembed-1 beats frozen BERT. Every decision actually made — which baseline is the bar, whether a fine-tune helped, whether an open-weight model can replace production — was a decision among the top six. That is exactly the region where the holdout is blind.

The rule: the holdout is a fine screen for “is this model broken”. It cannot rank models that work.

What changes

01

“Qwen3-Embedding-8B beats Voyage-4-large” is retracted

The claim in CLAUDE.md — the first model of any kind to beat Boardy's production model, 0.6595 vs 0.6086 — does not survive. On 200 pairs Qwen scores 0.5529 against Voyage-4-large's 0.5726, and loses on every retrieval metric (MRR 0.2045 vs 0.3102, R@1 0.0500 vs 0.1300). Its hard-negative AUC of 0.4680 is below chance — and hard negatives are the only population that exists in production.

Verified through the same baselines/hf_embedding code path, same 8192 context, no truncation. This run's own holdout subset lands at 0.6543 against the published 0.6595.

02

No open-weight model beats production overall

BGE-en-ICL is the closest and genuinely good at retrieval — MRR 0.3190 and R@1 0.1700 both beat Voyage-4-large — but it trails on pair AUC (0.5389 vs 0.5726) and recall@10. A real result worth carrying forward, but not the claim that was on the board.

03

TF-IDF's strength was a small-pool artifact

Pair AUC holds up (0.5649 vs 0.5922) but retrieval collapses as the pool grows: MRR 0.2475 → 0.1313, R@1 0.1379 → 0.0500. TF-IDF beating the fine-tunes was always a pair-classification result; it does not survive as a retrieval result.

04

The fine-tuned nano arms hold the top two spots

top1_ctrl (0.3550) and Arm A v2 (0.3341) lead all fourteen models, ahead of production. The 69-pair view had ranked Qwen micro-6 first; on 200 pairs it falls to sixth — the largest rank movement in the table.

Two defects found on the way

TF-IDF is not reproducible across environments

baselines/tfidf fits with max_features=20000, and on this data the vocabulary lands at exactly 20000 — the truncation binds. sklearn then drops features by document frequency, and that tie-break is not stable across environments. Identical code, data, scikit-learn 1.9.0 and numpy 2.4.6 give holdout 0.5922 / MRR 0.2475 in this repo's venv but 0.5914 / 0.2653 in a container. There are no ties in the score matrix — it is a genuinely different fitted vectorizer. TF-IDF's numbers here therefore come from a local run, the only environment reproducing the published row.

max_length must match the published run

An initial pass ran E5-Mistral and NV-Embed-v2 at 4096 where their published runs used 8192. E5's holdout drifted to 0.5819 from 0.5664; corrected to 8192 it reproduces exactly. NV-Embed additionally OOMs at 8192 on A100-40GB for the 200-pair text set and needs A100-80GB.

Caveats

  • 100 positive queries still quantizes recall@1 at 0.01. One- or two-query differences remain noise; the top-6 spread of 0.052 MRR is roughly five queries.
  • Retrieval metrics compare models within one population only — a 178-candidate pool is strictly harder than a 65-candidate one.
  • NV-Embed-v2 remains a documented approximation: it ships no sentence-transformers prompts dict and is run symmetrically, which understates it.
  • The hybrid TF-IDF+nano fusion and the LLM judge are not in this table. The fusion needs a fitted scorer rather than an encoder; the judge has no shared vector space, so no retrieval metrics.
  • This changes how models are ranked, not what the task is. Absolute numbers stay low because the task is residual “will these two connect”, not topical relevance.
