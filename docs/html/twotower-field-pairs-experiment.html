Do Two-Field Fine-Tunes Beat the Full-Profile Fine-Tune?

Only three profile fields carry real person-identity signal alone — positioning, background, lookingFor. Three isolated packages trim both seeker and candidate text to one such pair, then fine-tune top1_ctrl's exact recipe on it. None beat the full-profile fine-tune — but all three win on hard-negative resistance, and which pair wins flips once training and a trimmed candidate side enter the picture.

All 200 real pairs

background+lookingFor comes closest to top1_ctrl overall. Every trimmed arm beats it on hard-neg AUC, at the cost of easy-neg AUC and general retrieval.

Exact numbers

The ranking flipped once trained

A free eval-time sweep (frozen model, seeker side only trimmed, candidate kept as the full profile) ranked positioning+lookingFor best. Trim both sides and actually fine-tune, and background+lookingFor wins instead — on every metric.

Holdout misled again

Same pattern as every prior two-tower experiment in this project: the 69-pair holdout made background+lookingFor look like it had already beaten top1_ctrl. All 200 pulls it back.
