Can we tell which introductions people will actually accept?

The findings that mattered, in the order they changed what we believed.

July 16 — July 31, 2026
01  ·  Framing

Every pair was already recommended. The label is what humans did next.

A hundred accepted. A hundred declined. The negatives are production’s own false positives — plausible intros people still walked away from. And whatever we build must answer in under 100 ms.

The task

Not topical relevance. Residual fit: will these two actually connect.

02  ·  Lexical floor

Plain keyword overlap already gets you about halfway — then stalls.

Query↔match lexical overlap looks almost the same for accepts and declines. TF-IDF cosine lands near ~0.59 and plateaus, because both classes already cleared production’s relevance bar.

What that means

If a model only measures topic overlap, it is solving yesterday’s problem.

03  ·  Baselines

First we measured what already existed.

Frozen BERT was near chance. Voyage-4-nano did better. Voyage-4-large — Boardy’s production embedder — set the bar. Nothing gets called a win until it beats these, under the latency budget.

The decision

Accuracy alone is not enough. Serving must stay under 100 ms.

Matched holdout browser Embedding space
04  ·  Query ablation

Remove the search query and Voyage gets worse.

BERT barely noticed — it wasn’t using the ask anyway. Both Voyage models dropped on retrieval. The query is a small fraction of the seeker text, but it carries the ask that the biography dilutes.

Later confirmation

Encoding query and profile separately, then blending them, lifted recall without any training.

Query-weighted encoding Local writeup
05  ·  Synthetic generation

Two hundred real outcomes is not much to learn from.

So we built a pipeline that invents people and labeled intros overnight — hundreds of practice pairs, reviewed lightly, then promoted for training.

The bet

If we cannot buy more human outcomes, manufacture practice ones.

06  ·  LoRA run_001

The first fine-tune looked brilliant. It wasn’t.

Train-dev pair AUC near 0.99. On the real holdout: ~0.58 overall, and hard-negative AUC below chance. Worse than a coin flip on the cases production actually gets wrong.

The decision

Stop shipping numbers. Find what it really learned.

07  ·  Root cause

It read our handwriting, not human behaviour.

A classifier shown only the candidate’s profile guessed the synthetic label at 99.2% accuracy — and chance on real data. Arm A, trained on real pairs only, beat the synth-trained run with a fifth of the data.

The decision

Quarantine the batch. Never let the answer touch the text again.

08  ·  Safer generation

Invent the people first. Decide the match later.

Unlabeled profiles from Ollama and Bedrock — no pos/neg attached at write time. Pairing and judging became a separate step, so the generator could not smuggle the answer into the prose.

The invariant

Never let the label author the biography.

09  ·  LLM judge

Ask a model directly — and never give it the search query.

Gemini flash-lite beat Voyage-large on accept/decline, and uniquely did better on hard negatives than easy ones. That inversion is the tell: it is not scoring keyword overlap. Too slow to serve. Valuable as a labeler and distillation target.

Constraint

Per-candidate LLM calls are out under 100 ms. Keep the signal; move it into a bi-encoder.

10  ·  Embeddings

A free model seemed to beat production. Then we checked on all 200.

On the 69-pair holdout, Qwen3-Embedding-8B looked like a breakthrough. On the full 200 real pairs the claim reversed — Voyage still wins overall. The holdout ranks broken models well and strong ones almost at random.

Retracted · Jul 31

All-200 is the canonical leaderboard. No open-weight model beats Voyage overall; BGE-en-ICL is closest on retrieval.

11  ·  RRF pairing

Find with one family. Judge with another.

Dense + BM25 fused with RRF, then an LLM judge — so retrieval never grades its own ranking. Leakage probes dropped from catastrophic to merely cautious. The live weakness: some seekers are always rejected, so seeker identity alone predicts labels.

How to train

Within a seeker — comparisons that cancel base rate — not a plain pairwise classifier.

Triplet fine-tune Big-batch follow-up Ablation verdict
12  ·  Training recipe

One person, one good match, one bad match — many at once.

Triplet / contrastive training with a real corpus for checkpoint selection produced the best MRR in the project. Sharpening the loss to chase recall@1 backfired. Micro-batch size moved retrieval; an extra negative often hurt.

What held

Comparison learning under the latency budget beats isolated pair labels.

13  ·  Honest status

Safer generation fixed the handwriting leak. It did not fix the transfer gap.

Models trained on judge-labeled synthetic pairs still struggle to move real holdout numbers. Synthetic vocabulary can transfer; synthetic labels often teach the teacher. Scoring each lookingFor ask separately helped — the clever mixer less so.

Still open

Arm C-scale usable synth, distillation of the judge, and a real latency benchmark.

Where it stands · July 31

What we can say out loud.

The task is residual accept/decline among production’s own recommendations.
Hard negatives are the whole population. Keywords alone will never finish the job.
Voyage-4-large still leads overall on all 200. The Qwen holdout win is retracted.
Fine-tunes can lead retrieval (MRR). The LLM judge leads hard-neg discrimination — offline only.
Two-tower + comparison training fits the <100 ms budget.
Adapters merge free. Per-candidate LLMs and cross-encoders do not.
Usable synthetic data is still the open thread.
Leakage is fixed by construction. Teaching real outcomes from judge labels is not.
