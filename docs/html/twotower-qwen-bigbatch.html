Micro-batch 1 was never a measured limit

The previous Qwen fine-tune ran at micro-batch 1, citing an out-of-memory error. That error was from loading the model in fp32 on a 40 GB card — and the same config already fixed it twice over, with half-precision weights and gradient checkpointing. Nobody re-measured afterwards.

The result that overturns a headline

What this means for the project's leaderboard

Qwen3-8B's headline result — "beats Voyage-4-large, 0.6595 vs 0.6086" — rests entirely on the population where this bias is largest. On all 200 real pairs, frozen Qwen at full width scores 0.5420, below both frozen nano (0.5593) and Voyage-4-large (0.5726). The ranking reverses.

What survives, and gets stronger

Fine-tuning Qwen produces gains that do generalise: against its own frozen baseline on all 200 pairs, pair AUC +0.053, hard-negative AUC +0.104, MRR +0.100, recall@1 +0.100. Every one is far above the noise floor. The equivalent nano fine-tune moved +0.007 / +0.059 / +0.022 / +0.005 — essentially nothing. Fine-tuning the big model works; fine-tuning the small one didn't.

Read this one carefully

voyage-4-nano beats Voyage-4-large at top-1 retrieval by 5.5 queries out of 100, and the gap holds on all three populations. But the credit belongs to nano, not to fine-tuning — frozen nano scores 0.1800 and the fine-tuned version 0.1850, a difference of half a query. Meanwhile frozen Qwen puts the right person first in only 4 of 100 queries, despite having the strongest pair-scoring ability of any model here. It is good at judging a pair it is handed, and bad at finding one in a pool.

An asymmetry found late

The setting that shortens embedding vectors to 1024 numbers was inherited from the small model's config, where it does nothing — nano's vectors are natively 1024 long. Applied to Qwen, whose vectors are 4096 long, it throws away three quarters of each one.

ModelNative widthUsedEffect
voyage-4-nano10241024no truncation
Qwen3-Embedding-8B40961024cut to a quarter
voyage-4-large20481024requested at half width

A control run at Qwen's full 4096 width bounds the damage at +0.014 pair AUC on the 200-pair set — real, but far too small to change any conclusion above. It does mean every Qwen number here is measured with one hand tied, and that the fine-tuned arms have never been tested at full width at all. That is the clearest next experiment.

Limits worth keeping attached

  • One run per arm. There is no replicate-based noise floor for this experiment; the nano ablation's ±0.013–0.016 is the closest reference and doesn't strictly transfer.
  • Training labels are a language model's opinion on synthetic profiles, not real accept/decline outcomes. Only the evaluation is real.
  • Retrieval numbers can't be compared between populations — each ranks against a different-sized pool (178, 120, 65). A bigger pool is strictly harder.
  • Frozen Qwen at full width scores 0.6345 on the holdout against a published 0.6595, most likely a precision difference. Small, unexplained, and a reason not to mix these numbers with the older baseline table.
